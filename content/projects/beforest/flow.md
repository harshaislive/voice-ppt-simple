# Presentation Flow — Story-Driven

## The Arc
The presentation follows a 7-slide emotional journey:

1. **TENSION** — "You've been running at 100%. And calling it living." Hold up the mirror. Name the exhaustion.
2. **REFRAME** — "What if 90% was enough?" 30 nights. Not subtraction — protection. Not a holiday — a practice.
3. **PROOF** — "Seven years of quiet work." 6 collectives, 1,300 acres, 250 families. Earn their trust with substance.
4. **EXPERIENCE** — "Your life in the collectives." Not amenities — transformation. Belonging to a place.
5. **CLARITY** — "The structure. What it is and what it isn't." 30 person-nights, 10 years, any collective. Address objections directly. Person-nights, not family nights. No carry-forward — rhythm, not accumulation.
6. **URGENCY** — "Why now?" They've said 'next year' too many times. Every year they wait is a year of 10% they don't get back.
7. **ACTION** — "Experience before commitment." Book Blyton Bungalow. Feel it with your feet. The pilot, not the backup plan.

## Pacing
- Each slide is a scene, not a data point
- Immersive visuals with minimal on-screen text
- Narration carries the substance — slides carry the emotion
- Allow weight after key revelations

## Key Rules
- NEVER break down the price into per-night or per-day costs
- Frame the offer as: 30 nights per year for 10 years, 300 nights of intentional living
- Speak to one person, not an audience
- Use words like rhythm, practice, calibration, reset — not vacation, holiday, escape, getaway
- The trial stay at Blyton Bungalow is the pilot, not the consolation prize
- Both paths (full membership and trial stay) are honest and valid
- Close with conviction, not desperation
